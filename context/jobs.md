# Jobs to be done

What the buyer is actually trying to get done when they pick up a forecasting tool. Written from 31
discovery calls in H1 2026 (Priya's notes, tagged in the CRM).

## 1. Commit a number I can defend

"When I give the CEO a number on Monday, I want to know how it was built, so that when it moves I can
explain why rather than apologise." Most common job, every head of GTM. Our product's reason to exist.

## 2. Stop reconciling spreadsheets

RevOps job. "When the forecast call starts, I want the roll-up already done from CRM data, so that I spend
the meeting on deals and not on formulas." Measured in hours per week; the average on calls was nine.

## 3. Find out which reps I cannot trust

Said less politely in discovery. "When I look at a rep's commit, I want to know their historical accuracy,
so that I can weight it." Sensitive: this is why we never show rep accuracy to anyone but the leader.

## 4. Know where the pipeline came from and whether it was any good

Head of growth job. "When the quarter misses, I want to know whether the pipeline was bad or the execution
was bad, so that I fix the right thing." Needs segment-level history we only get after a quarter of data.

## 5. Get the board off my back about forecast accuracy

Appears from Series C. The board asks for a trailing forecast-accuracy chart; nobody has one. We produce it
from day one with CRM history, which is often the moment the deal turns.

## Jobs we do not serve (and say so)

Territory design, comp plans, cold outreach, contact data. People ask. We say no and point them elsewhere.
